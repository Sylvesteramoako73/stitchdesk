The surest way to stop missing December deadlines is to decide your capacity before the rush starts, set a firm cut off date for new orders, take a deposit on every order, and keep every order, measurement and promised date in one place you check every morning. Most missed deadlines are not about sewing speed. They come from taking on more than the workshop can finish and losing track of what was promised to whom.

## Work out how many orders you can really finish

Start with the days you have, not the orders you want. Count the working days between now and the last date you will hand over outfits, then subtract Sundays, church days and a buffer for power cuts. Multiply by what each person in your workshop can finish in a day for your most common styles. A kaba and slit and a beaded wedding gown are not the same job, so count in hours of work, not number of orders.

That number is your December capacity. Write it down. When you reach it, you are full.

## Set a cut off date and say it early

Pick the last day you will accept orders for Christmas and post it on your WhatsApp status and Instagram in October. Clients respect a clear date far more than a vague promise. A cut off also protects your regular clients, who should not lose their slot to a last minute order.

After the cut off, take orders only for January, and say so plainly.

## Take a deposit on every order

A deposit turns a maybe into a commitment. It covers your fabric and lining if you are buying them, and it stops clients from booking with three designers and collecting from one. Many designers ask for half up front. Whatever you choose, apply it to everyone and record it the moment it is paid, with the method, so there is no argument in January.

## Keep measurements where you can always find them

The December rush is when notebooks go missing. Keep each client's measurements with their name and phone number, dated, so you can see when they were last measured. If a client was measured more than a few months ago, ask them in for a quick re measure before you cut.

## Give every order a ready date and look at it every morning

Each order needs one promised ready date and, for fitted styles, a fitting date a few days before it. Every morning, look at three lists: orders that are overdue, orders due in the next seven days, and orders that are ready but not yet collected. Ten minutes with those lists will tell you what to cut and sew today.

## Tell clients before they ask

Most of the calls you get in December are clients asking if their outfit is ready. Send a short message when you take the order, a reminder before the fitting, and a message the day it is ready with the balance to bring. Better still, give each client a link where they can check their order themselves.
